Screen setup

1. Match a bank card to size the symbols

Hold any credit, debit or ID card flat against the screen inside the outline. Drag the slider until the outline matches the card edges exactly. Do this once on each phone, iPad or laptop.

card

2. Viewing distance

Symbol size scales to keep the same visual angle, so any distance works. Pick one you can repeat every time. Defaults: letters 36 inches (clinic standard), ring and plates 24 inches (about laptop distance).

3. Room and screen

  • Turn off color filters. On iPhone and iPad: Settings › Display & Brightness, turn off True Tone and Night Shift. Also check Accessibility › Display & Text Size and make sure Color Filters and Reduce White Point are off. On a laptop, turn off Night Light or f.lux.
  • Dark room. Room lights off, as at the clinic. Give your eyes a minute to settle.
  • Same brightness every time, around the middle, with auto-brightness off if you can. Changing it between runs makes scores harder to compare.
  • Prop the phone or iPad at your chosen distance, straight in front of you, and tap with one hand. A stand or a stack of books works.
  • Cover one eye with your palm for the ring and letter tests, without pressing on it. Wear your usual glasses or contacts, never tinted or color-correcting lenses.
  • Always guess. A guess is right 1 time in 4 for the ring and 1 time in 10 for letters. A blank answer is never right.

  • The FAA does not accept color-correcting lenses such as X-Chrom or EnChroma for testing.

What practice can and cannot change

Practice cannot change how the cones respond. On a properly run clinic test, the score mostly reflects actual color vision. Practice can remove avoidable misses: hesitating, not guessing, losing focus on faint symbols, or being thrown by the format. Scores also vary from run to run, which matters when a score sits near the pass line.

Home screens are not calibrated, so practice scores can differ from clinic scores by a few points. The ring test's adaptive method follows the published description, and the number plates are generated from the same principle as Waggoner plates rather than copied, so treat both scores as close estimates.
