Screen setup

1. Match a bank card to size the symbols

Hold any credit, debit or ID card flat against the screen inside the outline. Drag the slider until the outline matches the card edges exactly. You only need to do this once per device.

card

2. Viewing distance

The letter test is done at 36 inches. Published studies of the C-ring test used 60 cm, about 24 inches. Symbol size scales to keep the same visual angle, so any distance works. Pick one you can repeat every time.

3. Room and screen

  • Turn off color filters: Night Shift, True Tone, Night Light, f.lux, blue-light or dark-reader extensions. They shift the colors this page computes.
  • Dark room. Room lights off, as at the clinic. Give your eyes a minute to settle.
  • Same brightness every time, around the middle. Changing it between runs makes scores harder to compare.
  • Cover one eye with your palm without pressing on it. Wear your usual distance glasses or contacts, never tinted or color-correcting lenses.
  • Answer fast and always guess. A guess is right 1 time in 10 for letters and 1 time in 4 for the ring. A blank answer is never right.

How the clinic tests work

Letters (Rabin CCT)C ring (ColorDx CCT-HD)
SymbolSloan letters C D H K N O R S V Z inside a crosshairA ring with a gap facing up, down, left or right
AnswerRead the letter aloud, or the tech enters itPress the arrow that points to the gap, within 5 seconds
OrderEach eye alone: 20 red, then 20 green, then 20 blueEach eye alone: red, green and blue
ContrastFixed fade: 10 steps of 0.16 log units, 2 letters each. Red and green 27.5% to 1%, blue 173% to about 6%Adaptive: gets fainter after right answers and stronger after wrong ones, using a Bayesian method (Psi)
TimingEach letter shows 1.0 to 1.6 s, then blank gray for the same timeRing stays up to 5 s or until you answer
ScoreLetters correct × 5, from 0 to 100. Normal is 75 or moreThreshold converted to the same Rabin scale, from 0 to 175. Studies use 75 as the normal cutoff, and some use 90
  • The FAA lists the military version of the Rabin test with a pass of 55 or more in each color, and says a staircase version is allowed.
  • The FAA does not accept color-correcting lenses such as X-Chrom or EnChroma for testing.

What practice can and cannot change

Practice cannot change how the cones respond. On a properly run clinic test, the score mostly reflects actual color vision. What practice can fix are avoidable misses: hesitating past the time limit, not guessing, losing focus on faint symbols, or being surprised by the format.

Practice scores can differ from clinic scores by a few points, because home screens are not calibrated. The C-ring practice uses its own adaptive method modeled on the published description, so treat its score as a close estimate, not a copy of the device.
